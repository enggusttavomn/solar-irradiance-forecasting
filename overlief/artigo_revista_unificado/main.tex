\title{Multi-Resolution Global Horizontal Irradiance Forecasting at Electric-Vehicle Manufacturing Locations: TimesNet and Dilated Recurrent Networks}

\author[unicamp]{Gustavo de Melo Nascimento}
\author[unicamp]{Thales Wulfert Cabral}
\author[unicamp]{Gustavo Fraidenraich}
\address[unicamp]{School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Campinas, Campinas, SP, Brazil}

\begin{abstract}
This study presents a matched multi-resolution evaluation of global horizontal
irradiance (GHI) forecasting at ten electric-vehicle manufacturing locations.
TimesNet and DilatedRNN are compared with identical chronological splits,
forecast origins, inputs, post-processing, and location-first aggregation
within hourly, daily, and monthly tasks. At the terminal cumulative horizons,
TimesNet and DilatedRNN respectively obtained macro-MAEs of 52.21 and
52.96~W/m$^2$ at 72 hours, 44.08 and 43.85~W/m$^2$ at 30 days, 13.93
and 13.01~W/m$^2$ at one month, and 14.51 and 14.37~W/m$^2$ at six
months. TimesNet led the hourly macro-average at all checkpoints and the
daily comparison through 14 days; DilatedRNN led at 30 days and in the
principal one-month task. Location-level win counts sometimes reversed the
macro-average ranking, demonstrating that neither architecture was universally
superior. Daily and monthly learned models were evaluated over five seeds;
the hourly extension used the single official seed. All claims are descriptive
because adjacent multi-step targets overlap, and weather is post-hoc context.
\end{abstract}
