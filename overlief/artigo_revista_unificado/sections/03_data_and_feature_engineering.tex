\subsection{Data and Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:data}

\subsubsection{Forecasting data and spatial sampling}

The forecasting target is global horizontal irradiance (GHI), expressed as a
mean irradiance in W/m$^2$ at each temporal resolution. The underlying product
is the GOES Aggregated Physical Solar Model v4 distributed through the National
Solar Radiation Database (NSRDB) \cite{sengupta2018,nlr2026}. NSRDB irradiance
is a satellite-informed, physically modelled grid product; it is not a
measurement from a pyranometer installed at any factory. The factory
coordinates therefore serve only to select the associated NSRDB grid cell.
Throughout the article, the term \emph{location} denotes that
factory-associated grid point, not a plant-level measurement system.

The ten locations form a common panel covering Brazil, Mexico, and the United
States, but the temporal tasks use two independently acquired NSRDB archives.
The official hourly archive contains native 30-min records that its pipeline
aggregates to one-hour means. The daily archive was requested separately at a
60-min interval and exported as arithmetic civil-day means; monthly GHI is then
computed as the arithmetic mean of those daily values in each civil month.
Both archives cover the same ten factory-associated locations and the nominal
2019--2024 study period, but the daily/monthly records are not reaggregations of
the exact HDF5 records used by the hourly experiment. Differences caused by
independent retrieval resolution or product revision are therefore part of the
cross-task provenance and are one reason numerical errors are compared only
within a matched task. Observations from 2019 provide the initial context needed
by tasks whose first forecast origin occurs in 2020.

Table~\ref{tab:variables} distinguishes model inputs from indexing and
context-only fields. This separation is central to the matched comparison:
TimesNet, DilatedRNN, LSTM, and XGBoost receive the same causal GHI history and
the same site identifier. Calendar variables, coordinates, climate classes,
and meteorological descriptors are not supplied as predictors.

\subsubsection{Quality control and leakage control}

Each stored source is audited according to its schema before modelling.
Timestamp and GHI fields are parsed, the records are sorted chronologically,
and the resulting index must be unique, monotonic, regular, and common across
locations. Invalid timestamps, nonnumeric or non-finite GHI, negative GHI,
duplicates, discontinuous grids, and an inconsistent stored site identity are
rejected. Thus the daily loader repairs source row order by sorting rather than
claiming to reject a merely unsorted input. Product and retrieval metadata are
retained as provenance rather than supplied to the forecast models. Monthly
aggregation is performed only after the daily checks.

For a site $\ell$, the matched input at origin $t$ is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_{\ell,t}
 =
 \left(y_{\ell,t-c},\ldots,y_{\ell,t-1};\,\ell\right),
 \label{eq:matched_input}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the task-specific context length and $\ell$ is the fixed site
identifier. Every target vector starts at $t$, and window construction rejects
an origin if its complete target would cross the declared partition boundary.
Consequently, no lag, normalization parameter, or target value is obtained
from beyond the relevant cut.

Min--max parameters are estimated separately for each site. Model selection
uses scales fitted to observations strictly before 1 January 2023. After the
epoch count has been selected, the refitted model uses new scales fitted
strictly before 1 January 2024. The same parameters transform the corresponding
inputs and targets and invert the forecasts. Values outside the training range
are not clipped, so genuine extrapolation remains observable.

\subsubsection{Geographical and climatic context}

Table~\ref{tab:locations} reports the canonical factory coordinates and the
present K\"oppen--Geiger class sampled at those coordinates. Classes and their
confidence values come from the aligned 0.008333-degree (nominal 1-km) products
of Beck et al. \cite{beck2018}. Sampling uses the raster cell containing each
WGS84 point, without interpolation. The 0.5-degree product is used only as the
background of the Americas map and never to assign a site's class.

\subsubsection{Independent post-hoc meteorological context}

Daily NASA POWER fields for 2024 provide an external context layer for error
interpretation \cite{nasapowerdaily}. These are gridded estimates rather than
in-situ confirmation of weather at a factory. The retained variables are
corrected precipitation, cloud amount, all-sky surface shortwave irradiation,
and clear-sky surface shortwave irradiation in local solar time. The complete
table contains 366 dates for each of the ten locations.

The adverse-condition rules were declared independently of forecast error:
the author-defined operational tags are precipitation-indicated when corrected
precipitation is at least 1~mm/day, high-cloud when cloud amount is at least
80\%, and adverse when either condition holds. These thresholds are not
presented as universal meteorological definitions and are applied only after
forecasts and errors have been fixed. NASA POWER values are not joined to
training, validation, refit, or test input matrices and are not used to select a
model or metric. The join matches POWER local-solar-time dates to the fixed-
offset NSRDB local calendar and uses factory coordinates rather than the exact
NSRDB grid-cell centres. It therefore supports only an approximate, day-level,
post-hoc description; it neither confirms conditions at a particular forecast
hour nor supports causal attribution of forecast errors.
