\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evidence from the hourly source study}

Under the existing 72-hour hourly protocol, TimesNet achieved a macro-RMSE of
101.04~W/m$^2$, compared with 104.68~W/m$^2$ for the LSTM, a relative
reduction of 3.48\%. TimesNet also obtained lower RMSE than the LSTM at the
24- and 48-hour prefixes and lower RMSE than both temporal baselines at all
three prefixes. These values describe the hourly source experiment only.

\subsection{Evidence from the monthly source study}

In the existing one-month-ahead experiment, the consolidated DilatedRNN
forecast achieved a macro-MAE of 13.42~W/m$^2$, an RMSE of 16.20~W/m$^2$,
and an $R^2$ of 0.898. It ranked first among five evaluated methods by
macro-MAE, but led at only four of the ten locations. Local MAE ranged from
7.45 to 20.92~W/m$^2$. These values describe the monthly source experiment
only and are not numerically compared with the hourly TimesNet errors.

\subsection{Matched multi-resolution performance}

Table~\ref{tab:desempenho-macro-auditavel} reports the equal-site-weighted
macro-MAE for every cumulative checkpoint in the matched protocol. XGBoost
ranked first at every daily and hourly checkpoint, with 30-day and 72-hour
MAEs of 43.93 and 50.50~W/m$^2$, respectively. Calendar climatology ranked
first in both monthly tasks, reaching 12.57~W/m$^2$ at one month and
13.03~W/m$^2$ over the six-month prefix. These baseline results show that the
architecture comparison should not be read as a claim that either neural
model dominates the complete benchmark set.

\subsection{TimesNet--DilatedRNN comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:vitorias-timesnet-dilatedrnn} gives the site-paired comparison;
a negative mean or median difference denotes lower TimesNet MAE. TimesNet had
the lower macro-MAE at all hourly checkpoints, by 1.65, 0.29, and
0.75~W/m$^2$ at 24, 48, and 72 hours. Its site wins nevertheless fell from
eight at 24 hours to four at both 48 and 72 hours, so a minority of larger
TimesNet gains determined the later macro averages.

In the daily task, TimesNet led by 0.07--0.17~W/m$^2$ through seven days and
by 0.02~W/m$^2$ at 14 days. At 30 days, DilatedRNN led by
0.23~W/m$^2$ and won six sites. DilatedRNN also led the principal one-month
task by 0.93~W/m$^2$ and won six sites. In the exploratory six-month task,
the models were effectively tied at its one-month prefix (a
0.005~W/m$^2$ TimesNet advantage and five wins each); DilatedRNN led by
0.23 and 0.14~W/m$^2$ at three and six months and won seven sites at the
terminal horizon. These changes in sign and win count rule out a universal
architecture ranking.

\subsection{Variability across seeds}

Daily and monthly estimates use five independent seeds. As shown in
Table~\ref{tab:variabilidade-sementes}, the 30-day seed-level MAE was
$44.29\pm0.29$~W/m$^2$ for TimesNet and $44.10\pm0.60$~W/m$^2$ for
DilatedRNN. At one month it was $14.61\pm0.76$ versus
$13.45\pm0.35$~W/m$^2$, respectively. The exploratory six-month values were
$15.29\pm0.81$ and $14.77\pm0.45$~W/m$^2$. The hourly rows contain one
seed and therefore do not estimate seed variability.

\subsection{Favorable and unfavorable cases}

The global case rule was fixed before meteorological annotation: for every
site, origin, and cumulative horizon, it selects the maximum and minimum of
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{DilatedRNN}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{TimesNet}}$.
The strongest TimesNet gain occurred for GM Factory Zero at the 24-hour
forecast from 31 March: 46.30 versus 109.90~W/m$^2$, a
63.61~W/m$^2$ advantage. Post-hoc NASA POWER context described 99.64\% mean
cloud amount and 0.80~mm precipitation. The strongest deficit occurred for
Tesla Gigafactory Texas at the 24-hour forecast from 28 March: 150.82 versus
73.92~W/m$^2$, a 76.90~W/m$^2$ TimesNet disadvantage, with no independently
flagged adverse day. Both are hourly single-seed cases and illustrate why
selected examples cannot replace the full aggregate evaluation.

\subsection{Geographic and climatic interpretation}

Figure~\ref{fig:geographic_context} locates the ten sites in their descriptive
K\"oppen--Geiger context. With only ten purposively selected locations,
apparent differences between zones cannot establish a causal climatic effect.
Sites in the same country may remain far apart and occupy distinct regimes,
while nearby sites may share correlated weather.
