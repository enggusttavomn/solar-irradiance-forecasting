\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study completed a common, leakage-controlled evaluation of TimesNet and
DilatedRNN across hourly, daily, and monthly GHI forecasting tasks at ten
electric-vehicle manufacturing locations. The matched design fixes inputs,
chronological partitions, forecast origins, post-processing, metrics, and
location weights within each task, while retaining the different meanings of
hourly, daily, and monthly targets.

No architecture was universally superior. TimesNet had the lower macro-MAE at
all hourly checkpoints and through 14 days in the daily task. DilatedRNN led at
30 days, in the principal one-month task, and at the three- and six-month
prefixes of the exploratory task. Terminal location wins favored DilatedRNN at
six of ten hourly, daily, and one-month sites and seven of ten six-month sites,
even when a few larger TimesNet gains reversed a macro average. XGBoost led the
full benchmark set in the daily and hourly tasks, whereas calendar climatology
led the monthly tasks; consequently, the neural comparison does not imply
benchmark-wide dominance.

Five-seed daily and monthly results support the observed ranking patterns, but
the hourly extension has only the official seed 42 and provides no seed-level
uncertainty estimate. Overlapping multi-step targets further restrict the
paired evidence to descriptive site-level comparisons. The globally strongest
TimesNet gain and deficit both occurred in 24-hour forecasts and had opposite
weather contexts, reinforcing that post-hoc meteorology cannot be treated as a
causal explanation.

Future work should evaluate prospective years and unseen sites, repeat the
hourly extension over multiple seeds, and test whether causal exogenous inputs
such as numerical weather prediction and cloud-motion forecasts improve on the
strong tabular and climatological references. The released contracts,
forecast-origin outputs, seed-level metrics, context manifests, and SHA-256
artifact manifest provide the basis for those extensions.
