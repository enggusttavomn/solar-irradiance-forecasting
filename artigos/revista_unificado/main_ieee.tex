\begin{abstract}
This study compares TimesNet and dilated recurrent neural networks
(DilatedRNN) for global horizontal irradiance (GHI) forecasting at ten
locations associated with electric-vehicle factories. Four hourly, daily,
and monthly tasks align causal GHI inputs, forecast origins, fitting cuts,
post-processing, and equal-site-weighted evaluation within each task.
At the terminal cumulative horizons, TimesNet achieved lower macro-MAE at
72 hours (52.48 versus 52.96~W/m$^2$), while DilatedRNN led at
30 days (43.85 versus 44.08~W/m$^2$), one month
(13.01 versus 13.89~W/m$^2$), and the exploratory six-month horizon
(14.37 versus 14.46~W/m$^2$). The one-month DilatedRNN reduction was
6.38\% relative to TimesNet; shorter-prefix rankings varied with horizon.
Daily and monthly results use five-seed ensembles, while the hourly
comparison retains seed 42. Site-level wins sometimes disagree with the
macro ranking, and several ensemble gaps are small relative to seed
variability. The full reference-model benchmark is retained separately
and shows that winning the focal comparison does not imply superiority
over all evaluated models. Adapted procedural pseudocode and seven-day
forecast profiles support reproducibility and interpretation.
The evidence supports task-specific winners, with descriptive conclusions
limited by overlapping multi-step targets and a single test year.
Meteorology is used solely as independent post-hoc context.
\end{abstract}
