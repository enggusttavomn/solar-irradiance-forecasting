\begin{abstract}
This study presents an origin-aligned multi-resolution evaluation of global
horizontal irradiance (GHI) forecasting at ten locations associated with
electric-vehicle factories. Hourly, daily, and monthly tasks share
chronological splits, forecast origins, causal GHI inputs, physical
post-processing, and location-first aggregation within each resolution.
The complete benchmark includes three deterministic references, XGBoost,
LSTM, TimesNet, and DilatedRNN. XGBoost achieved the lowest macro-MAE in the
hourly and daily tasks (50.50~W/m$^2$ at 72 hours and 43.93~W/m$^2$ at
30 days), whereas calendar climatology led the monthly tasks (12.57~W/m$^2$
at one month and 13.03~W/m$^2$ over six months). In the focused architecture
comparison, TimesNet and DilatedRNN respectively obtained 52.48 and
52.96~W/m$^2$ at 72 hours, 44.08 and 43.85~W/m$^2$ at 30 days, 13.89
and 13.01~W/m$^2$ at one month, and 14.46 and 14.37~W/m$^2$ at six
months. TimesNet led at 24 and 72 hours and through 14 days in the daily
comparison; DilatedRNN led marginally at 48 hours, at 30 days, and in the
principal one-month task.
Location-level wins sometimes reversed the macro-average ranking, so neither
architecture was universally superior. Daily and monthly learned models use
five-seed ensembles; the hourly extension uses the single registered seed 42.
Claims remain descriptive because adjacent multi-step targets overlap, and
meteorology is used only as independent post-hoc context.
\end{abstract}
