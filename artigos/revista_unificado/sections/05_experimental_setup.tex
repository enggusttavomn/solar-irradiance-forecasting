\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

The four forecasting tasks appear in Block A of Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.
The Supplementary Material summarizes their context lengths, outputs,
evaluation horizons, and origin counts. Within each task, all models share
sites, origins, targets, fitting cutoffs, and post-processing. MAE and RMSE
are reported in W/m$^2$ and MSE in (W/m$^2$)$^2$ at every resolution, but
they describe different temporal aggregates and are interpreted separately
across tasks.

\subsubsection{Common matched protocol}

Each task evaluates the two focal architectures against a common benchmark:
three deterministic references (persistence, annual seasonal na\"ive, and,
for the daily and monthly tasks, calendar climatology), site-wise ARIMA, and
five global reference learners (XGBoost, LSTM, GRU, Transformer, and
Informer), specified in Section~\ref{sec:additional_baselines}. Global
learned models receive only causal GHI and site identity. In the daily and
monthly tasks, every stochastic learned model is fitted under the five seeds
11, 23, 42, 67, and 89. For each, we evaluate both individual-seed forecasts
and their arithmetic-mean ensemble, applying the post-processing in
Section~\ref{sec:methodology}. Deterministic references and ARIMA are
evaluated once. The primary comparison uses macro-MAE, calculated by
averaging site-level MAEs with equal weights. Complementary results include
MSE, RMSE, nRMSE, and $R^2$ at cumulative horizons and exact leads.

For all neural models, the selection and fresh-refit procedure in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:matched-training} uses early stopping on 2023
site-normalized validation MSE. Initial training uses the pre-2023 windows
(labeled ``adjustment'' in the saved artifacts); the selected epoch count governs
the same-seed refit on pre-2024 windows. This selection objective differs from
the primary test ranking by macro-MAE in physical units. Tree ensembles use
separate initial-training and refit instances with a fixed tree budget, and
ARIMA orders are selected on 2023 and refitted before the test. Only
refitted instances generate test forecasts.

Block B of Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes this selection and refitting
sequence.

Forecasts follow the rolling-origin protocol in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.
Hourly forecasts are issued once per day at local midnight, daily forecasts
advance one origin per day, and monthly forecasts advance one origin per
civil month.

\subsubsection{Hourly protocol and extensions}

The official hourly task uses 336 past hours (14 days) and produces the next
72 hours directly. The reported checkpoints are 24, 48, and 72 hours; they are
prefixes and exact leads extracted from the same 72-hour output, not separately
trained models. Initial training origins run from 15 January 2019 through
29 December 2022 (1,445 per site), and validation origins run from
1 January through 29 December 2023 (363 per site). The pre-test refit combines
origins from 15 January 2019 through 29 December 2023 (1,810 per site). The
retrospective test contains 363 origins from 1 January through
28 December 2024. The last 72-hour window ends at 23:00 on 30 December in the
fixed local-time index; corresponding UTC timestamps can extend into
31 December for western locations.

All hourly learned models use seed 42. Persistence, annual seasonal
na\"ive, XGBoost, LSTM, and TimesNet forecasts come from the official hourly
run. DilatedRNN, ARIMA, GRU, Transformer, and Informer are added with the
same 336-hour context and 72-hour output. Their forecasts are matched
one-to-one with the existing predictions by site, origin, target timestamp,
and lead. Target equality and input hashes are checked to ensure that the
extensions preserve the original data and comparator forecasts.

\subsubsection{Daily protocol}

The daily primary task uses 365 daily means to predict a 30-day vector. Metrics
are evaluated at 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 days. Initial training origins extend from
1 January 2020 through 2 December 2022, with targets ending no later than
31 December 2022 (1,067 origins per site). Validation uses
1 January--2 December 2023 (336 origins), and refit uses
1 January 2020--2 December 2023 (1,432 origins). The 2024 test uses
1 January--2 December and contains 337 complete origins per site because 2024
is a leap year.

\subsubsection{Monthly primary and exploratory protocols}

Civil-month means are calculated from the audited daily series. The primary
monthly task uses 12 months to predict the next month. It has 36 initial training
origins in 2020--2022, 12 validation origins in 2023, 48 refit origins in
2020--2023, and 12 test origins in 2024, for every site.

The exploratory monthly task uses the same 12-month context and directly
predicts six months. Its declared checkpoints are months 1, 3, and 6.
The final origin in each partition must fall no later than July so that all
six targets remain inside its final year. Initial training therefore contains
31 consecutive monthly origins from January 2020 through July 2022, validation
contains seven from January through July 2023, refit contains 43 from January
2020 through July 2023, and test contains seven from January through July 2024.
Earlier complete years in initial training and refit retain all twelve origins.
This task is exploratory because each site contributes
only seven test origins, each with a six-month output.

For the six-month task, exact leads are evaluated on different target-month
sets: lead 1 covers January--July, whereas lead 6 covers June--December. Its
horizon profile therefore combines lead-time and seasonal effects and is not
interpreted as an isolated degradation curve.

\subsubsection{Model capacity and computational evidence}

Table~\ref{tab:model_capacity} reports the number of trainable scalar
parameters in each neural model, including biases and the ten-site embedding.
The counts are calculated from the implemented module dimensions. TimesNet has a task-dependent direct temporal
projection, so its parameter count grows with both context and output length.
Dilated recurrence changes the temporal connections without adding separate
weights for each skipped position. The Transformer and Informer project each
time step to a single output, so their counts do not change across tasks.
The capacities of the tree ensemble and ARIMA are described by their
settings in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters}.
