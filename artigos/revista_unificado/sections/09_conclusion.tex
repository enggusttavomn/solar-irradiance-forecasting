\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DilatedRNN achieved lower cumulative macro-MAE than TimesNet at the full
forecast horizon in three of the four evaluated tasks. Its largest
relative advantage was in one-month forecasting, with
13.01 versus 13.89~W/m$^2$ and a 6.38\% reduction. In that task,
DilatedRNN also achieved lower MAE at six of the ten locations and in
four of five paired seed runs. Together, these results favor DilatedRNN
among the two implementations for the evaluated daily and monthly
full-horizon forecasts, with the strongest evidence at one month.

The 30-day and six-month ensemble advantages were
0.23 and 0.09~W/m$^2$, respectively, providing smaller margins than the
primary monthly result. TimesNet led the complete 72-hour hourly task
with 52.48 versus 52.96~W/m$^2$, and several intermediate horizons
also favored TimesNet. The three task wins are a descriptive synthesis
across distinct forecasting problems, rather than a pooled performance
score. DilatedRNN's lower parameter counts in all four configurations
add a model-size consideration to the choice; computational efficiency
was not measured.

The comparison controls inputs, temporal partitions, forecast origins,
post-processing, and site weights within each task. Its ten
factory-associated locations represent NSRDB solar-resource grid cells,
without quantifying factory demand or photovoltaic output. The
single hourly seed, overlapping targets, one test year, small
exploratory monthly sample, and unequal parameter budgets limit
generalization. The preference is specific to TimesNet versus DilatedRNN;
the complete reference-model benchmark in the Supplementary Material
documents stronger alternatives in several tasks.

The next step is to test whether DilatedRNN's monthly advantage persists
across additional years and unseen sites, and to separate the effects of
architecture, normalization, and model size. Multiple hourly seeds,
daylight-only metrics, measured computational costs, and weather forecasts
available at issuance would further refine the choice.
Under the present protocol, DilatedRNN is the favored candidate for
daily and monthly full-horizon forecasting, while TimesNet retains the
advantage for the full hourly trajectory.
