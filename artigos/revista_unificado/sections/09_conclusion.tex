\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study compared TimesNet and DilatedRNN on matched hourly, daily, and
monthly GHI tasks at ten factory-associated locations. Their causal GHI
inputs, partitions, origins, post-processing, metrics, and location weights
were controlled within each task. The results describe forecasts of the
NSRDB solar resource, without quantifying factory demand or photovoltaic
output.

For cumulative macro-MAE, TimesNet led the architecture comparison at 24 and
72 hours and through 14 days; DilatedRNN led at 48 hours, 30 days, the primary
one-month task, and the three- and six-month exploratory prefixes. Local
win counts sometimes disagreed with the macro ranking. At the terminal
horizons, DilatedRNN won six of ten hourly, daily, and one-month sites and
seven of ten six-month sites.

The complete benchmark gives a more qualified outcome. XGBoost led all
hourly checkpoints and daily checkpoints 1, 3, and 14; LSTM led at seven days,
and DilatedRNN led at 30 days with 43.85~W/m$^2$, compared with
43.93~W/m$^2$ for XGBoost. Calendar climatology led the monthly checkpoints.
Thus, neither neural architecture was consistently best across tasks.

Daily and monthly estimates use five-seed ensembles, and several differences
are small relative to the observed seed variability. The single hourly seed,
overlapping targets, one test year, and unequal parameter budgets limit
generalization. Post-hoc weather cases provide context but do not explain
model differences causally.

Future evaluation should include prospective years and unseen sites,
multiple hourly seeds, daylight-only metrics, measured computational cost,
and weather forecasts available at issuance. The retained contracts,
origin-level predictions, and integrity manifests provide a basis for
testing those extensions against the tabular and climatological references;
public release of the corrected forecast bundle remains necessary for
independent reconstruction of all reported metrics.
