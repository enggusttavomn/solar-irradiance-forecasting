\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Matched multi-resolution benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:desempenho-macro-auditavel} reports the equal-site-weighted
macro-MAE for every cumulative checkpoint in the matched protocol. XGBoost
ranked first at every daily and hourly checkpoint, with 30-day and 72-hour
MAEs of 43.93 and 50.50~W/m$^2$, respectively. Calendar climatology ranked
first in both monthly tasks, reaching 12.57~W/m$^2$ at one month and
13.03~W/m$^2$ over the six-month prefix. These baseline results show that the
architecture comparison should not be read as a claim that either neural
model dominates the complete benchmark set.

\input{tables/desempenho_macro}

Table~\ref{tab:metricas-terminais} complements the ranking metric with RMSE,
nRMSE, and $R^2$ for the terminal TimesNet--DilatedRNN comparisons. At
72 hours, for example, TimesNet obtained an RMSE of 101.17~W/m$^2$ versus
104.93~W/m$^2$ for DilatedRNN. These values belong to the same matched test
protocol as the MAE results and therefore supersede preliminary source-study
values for the unified comparison.

\input{tables/metricas_terminais}

The strongest result is therefore not a universal advantage of either neural
architecture. Under the controlled GHI-only information set, the fixed
XGBoost configuration provided the best fine-resolution benchmark, while the
calendar mean remained difficult to improve upon with the short monthly
history. These outcomes do not establish why a method won, but they show that
architectural sophistication alone did not determine forecast skill. They
also justify retaining the complete benchmark before examining the narrower
TimesNet--DilatedRNN contrast.

\subsection{TimesNet--DilatedRNN comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:vitorias-timesnet-dilatedrnn} gives the site-paired comparison;
a negative mean or median difference denotes lower TimesNet MAE. TimesNet had
the lower macro-MAE at 24 and 72 hours, by 1.34 and 0.48~W/m$^2$,
respectively; DilatedRNN led by 0.01~W/m$^2$ at 48 hours. TimesNet won seven
sites at 24 hours and four at both 48 and 72 hours, so the later macro averages
were determined by the magnitude rather than the count of local gains.

In the daily task, TimesNet led by 0.07--0.17~W/m$^2$ through seven days and
by 0.02~W/m$^2$ at 14 days. At 30 days, DilatedRNN led by
0.23~W/m$^2$ and won six sites. DilatedRNN also led the principal one-month
task by 0.89~W/m$^2$ and won six sites. In the exploratory six-month task,
TimesNet led the one-month prefix by 0.03~W/m$^2$ despite winning only four
sites; DilatedRNN led by 0.16 and 0.09~W/m$^2$ at three and six months and won
seven sites at the terminal horizon. These changes in sign and win count rule
out a universal architecture ranking.

\subsection{Variability across seeds}

Daily and monthly estimates use five independent seeds. In the exact records
retained in the Supplementary Material, the 30-day seed-level MAE was
$44.29\pm0.29$~W/m$^2$ for TimesNet and $44.10\pm0.60$~W/m$^2$ for
DilatedRNN. At one month it was $14.62\pm0.74$ versus
$13.45\pm0.35$~W/m$^2$, respectively. The exploratory six-month values were
$15.24\pm0.76$ and $14.77\pm0.45$~W/m$^2$. The hourly rows contain one
seed and therefore do not estimate seed variability. Figure~\ref{fig:seed_variability}
summarizes the mean and one standard
deviation of seed-level metrics. These values need not equal
Table~\ref{tab:desempenho-macro-auditavel}, which evaluates the arithmetic
ensemble forecast rather than averaging the nonlinear seed-level metric.

The scale of the architecture differences must be read together with this
variability. At 30 days, the 0.23~W/m$^2$ ensemble advantage of DilatedRNN is
smaller than either model's seed-level standard deviation. The one-month
advantage of 0.89~W/m$^2$ is larger, but still accompanies overlapping
seed-level ranges. At six months, the 0.09~W/m$^2$ terminal difference is again
small relative to seed variability. These comparisons are descriptive rather
than confidence intervals, but they prevent small changes in rank from being
presented as stable architecture dominance.

\subsection{Relation to the source protocols}

The protocol-specific studies that motivated the unified design remain useful
as lineage evidence, but not as a head-to-head comparison. The present audit
reruns the architecture comparison under common inputs, origins,
post-processing, aggregation, and terminal metrics. Because the source studies
used different targets, competitors, and evaluation rules, their preliminary
error values are not carried into the matched rankings above.

\subsection{Favorable and unfavorable cases}

The global case rule was fixed before meteorological annotation: for every
site, origin, and cumulative horizon, it selects the maximum and minimum of
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{DilatedRNN}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{TimesNet}}$.
The strongest TimesNet gain occurred for GM Factory Zero at the 24-hour
forecast from 31 March: 45.43 versus 109.90~W/m$^2$, a
64.47~W/m$^2$ advantage. Post-hoc NASA POWER context described 99.64\% mean
cloud amount and 0.80~mm precipitation. The strongest deficit occurred for
Tesla Gigafactory Texas at the 24-hour forecast from 28 March: 150.02 versus
73.92~W/m$^2$, a 76.10~W/m$^2$ TimesNet disadvantage, with no independently
flagged adverse day. Both are hourly single-seed cases and illustrate why selected examples cannot
replace the full aggregate evaluation. Figure~\ref{fig:contrasting_cases}
retains the model MAEs, signed advantage, independent weather annotation, and
the corresponding forecast trajectories in one view.

\subsection{Geographic and climatic interpretation}

Figure~\ref{fig:geographic_context} locates the ten sites in their descriptive
K\"oppen--Geiger context. With only ten purposively selected locations,
apparent differences between zones cannot establish a causal climatic effect.
Sites in the same country may remain far apart and occupy distinct regimes,
while nearby sites may share correlated weather.

As an error-independent check, the predeclared meteorological rule selected
Hyundai Metaplant Georgia on 5 August 2024, with 82.66~mm/day corrected
precipitation and 99.47\% cloud amount. This case was selected without
consulting either model's error and is descriptive rather than causal. Its
complete context record is retained in the Supplementary Material.
